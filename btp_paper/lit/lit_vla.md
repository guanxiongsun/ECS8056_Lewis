# Literature review A: evaluations and probes of language use in VLAs (2023 to 2026)

Prepared 2 Oct 2026 for the anonymous BtP workshop paper (CoRL 2026, "Everything Beneath the Policy").
Companion BibTeX: `lit_vla.bib` (79 verified entries).

**How this was compiled.** Every paper listed here was checked against a primary source fetched on 2 Oct 2026: the arXiv API record (title, authors, dates, comment, journal-ref), and for the papers marked "Read" the arXiv HTML rendering, which was parsed and searched for the details reported below. Venue pages (PMLR, ACL Anthology, NeurIPS proceedings, RSS proceedings) were fetched for the published entries marked as such. Quotations are copied from the fetched text. I searched with 25 arXiv API queries (logged in section (h)) and about 20 web searches, including venue checks. The Semantic Scholar API returned HTTP 429 (rate limit) on every attempt, so it did not contribute; coverage of non-arXiv venues relies on web search. "Read" in the tables means the full text was searched; "Abs." means only the arXiv abstract was read.

Conventions: "twins" means two identical objects in one scene; "same-side" means both twins on the same side of the gripper; "real frames" means camera images from a physical robot (not simulator renders); "action-level readout" means the evaluation reads the model's action output (or its token distribution) directly at a fixed observation, rather than only scoring closed-loop task success.

## (a) Synthesis

**What prior work measures.** Between 2024 and September 2026, the dominant way to ask whether a VLA uses language has been to perturb the instruction and count closed-loop successes in simulation, mostly in LIBERO or SimplerEnv. Perturbations include LLM paraphrases (LADEV, VLATest, LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-PRO, LIBERO-Para), blank or garbled instructions (LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-PRO), counterfactual target or goal swaps in a fixed layout (LIBERO-CF, LangGap, InstructMove, BeTTER), contradictory or impossible instructions (ICBench, ConflictVLA-Bench, "Do What?"), other languages (Kirouane et al.; Dong et al.; Chen et al., "Beyond English") and adversarial or red-teamed prompts (Jones et al.; Q-DIG; DAERT). Real-robot components are small (typically 10 to 30 trials per condition). The headline verdict is that VLAs "tend to ignore language instructions completely" (LIBERO-Plus), "largely ignore language instructions" (LangGap), or that "vision overrides language" (LIBERO-CF). A smaller second line of work reads the model directly at a fixed observation: STAGE reads OpenVLA's single-step 7-DoF action under instruction swaps, Act2Answer and several interpretability papers fit linear probes to hidden states (Lu et al.; Grant et al.; VLA-Trace; Chen et al., "When Instructions Retrieve Trajectories"), and steering papers intervene on activations (Häon et al.; Swann et al.; Buurmeijer et al.). This second line complicates the headline. STAGE finds that semantics are linearly decodable from OpenVLA but weakly expressed in its actions. Grant et al. find that language is ignored when the scene determines the task and used when it does not. Chen et al. conclude that "language is not simply ignored". VLATest even reports that OpenVLA did better under paraphrased instructions on two of four tasks. So the verdict already depends on stimulus design. No prior work asks how it depends on the measurement.

**Spatial words and identical objects.** Most work tests spatial language through anchor relations between distinct objects (STAGE: "the object to the right of the black bowl"; InstructMove's "Pick Spatial"; RoboIRGBench's spatial references) or through colour-coded objects at fixed positions (BeTTER: top/bottom crossed with red/blue). Same-category instances appear in LangGap's "Spatial Description" dimension (the two LIBERO-Spatial bowls, distinguished by anchor relations, 5 tasks). Truly identical twins appear in two places. The first is LIBERO-CF's real-robot spatial scene: three identical white cups at middle/left/right, with π0.5 fine-tuned on 20 demonstrations for the in-domain target and one each for the others, so the left/right test also measures training frequency. The second is RefGuard's duplicate produce pairs, where fine-tuned GR00T and π0.5 baselines "switch identity in 33-46%" of ambiguity trials. None of these works controls whether both twins sit on the same side of the gripper. That is the condition that separates "move toward the named side" from "move toward the object on the named side". Frames of reference are acknowledged as a problem for spatial words: COMFORT shows VLMs handle them inconsistently, RefGuard treats the frame as a latent variable, and InstructMove and RoboIRGBench define left/right in the robot frame. However, no VLA language study reports the axis and sign convention of the action readout itself, which is where our "lateral slot and sign" pitfall sits. That such conventions silently change outcomes is documented for control pipelines (vla-eval; Hochedel & Lalonde), not for language verdicts.

**Which measurement choices are reported.** Trial counts are nearly always given (10 to 50 per task or instruction). Seeds and intervals appear in a minority:
- STAGE: bootstrap 95% CIs with a stated seed.
- Act2Answer: a Wald chance band.
- Chen et al.: episode-bootstrap and Wilson intervals.
- Grant et al.: Wilson intervals.
- VLAQuantBench: Wilson and task-clustered intervals.
- IndustrialVLA-Bench, Kirouane et al. and RoboIRGBench: three seeds.

The readout rule is almost never stated. STAGE reads the "decoded native continuous action", ConflictVLA follows each model's "native inference protocol", and LIBERO-CF uses "officially released weights"; none says whether actions are argmax-decoded, sampled or expected values. Numerical precision is reported by the OpenVLA paper (bfloat16 default, with 4-bit matching bfloat16 success on 8 BridgeData V2 tasks). Three deployment studies show that precision and runtime can move closed-loop success by tens of points:
- VLAQuantBench: quantization recipes interact.
- The SmolVLA PyTorch-versus-ONNX study: requested FP16/INT8 exports were in fact FP32 graphs. LIBERO-Spatial success fell from 70% (PyTorch) to 41% (ONNX), and widening the exported language input from 16 to 24 tokens restored 75%.
- Adversarial weight-level bit-flip studies, where a few flipped bits in quantized weights reduce success to 0%.

None of these three examines a language verdict. Stimulus screening is reported by LIBERO-Plus, which removed tasks "solved by all models, or by a large majority". Mirror scoring is treated explicitly only by Act2Answer, which averages original and left/right-swapped layouts and notes that treating the two views as independent is "mildly anticonservative". The decisive statistic is discussed in four places:
- STAGE separates direction-blind "action sensitivity" from direction-aware "ActCheck".
- LIBERO-Para argues binary success overstates paraphrase robustness by 8 to 22%.
- "Robust Skills, Brittle Grounding" asks for decomposed metrics.
- The Counterfactual Memory Audit notes that action change is not evidence of guidance.

**Has anyone audited how measurement choices change verdicts?** The nearest work falls into four groups:
1. *Kirouane et al. (Sept 2026)* audit the instruments used to claim that a Cosmos3 VLA follows Greek. They find that "several plausible instruments produce false conclusions" (a benchmark that scores 84.6% under correct and 82.6% under wrong instructions; seed variation; training loss). They retract five of their own conclusions. Their study concerns closed-loop success instruments, not readout choices.
2. *Pipeline audits.* vla-eval finds single undocumented parameters that shift success by up to 55 points, including action-mode, quaternion and crop conventions. IndustrialVLA-Bench argues a score difference "can therefore reflect either policy capability or the evaluation path" and reports evidence tiers. Chen et al. (App. B) report a silent fault in a language pipeline. LIBERO-PRO's released code "reads the instruction from the file name, so the policy receives the original instruction while the success predicate scores the perturbed task" in its Semantic and Task cells, and the maintainers acknowledged it. I have not checked the issue or LIBERO-PRO's published numbers.
3. *Single-choice robustness checks.* STAGE adds a threshold-free AUC. Act2Answer runs rendering, tile-size and lighting ablations and states that conclusions are unchanged. Lin et al. report a cross-policy geometry ordering over nine representations.
4. *Verdict transfer outside manipulation.* A driving "check-up" (Yang et al.) asks which verdicts transfer from left- to right-hand traffic.

Outside VLAs, RL and robot-learning methodology papers show that seeds, statistics and design choices change conclusions (Henderson et al.; Agarwal et al.; Mandlekar et al.; Kress-Gazit et al.; Snyder et al.). ML has a multiverse framework (Bell et al.). I found no paper that crosses inference-time readout and analysis choices for a VLA language verdict, and no multiverse or specification-curve analysis of any robot-learning or VLA evaluation.

**Implications for our paper.** The gap holds if it is stated narrowly (section (d)). The paper should present itself as a measurement audit of a model organism, not as another "VLAs ignore language" benchmark. STAGE should be cited as the protocol neighbour: fixed observation, single-step OpenVLA readout, spatial-relation swaps, paraphrase controls and a direction-aware metric. We must state that it already separates direction-blind from direction-aware scores, and then say what we add: twins, same-side controls, real frames for OpenVLA, token-distribution readouts, and a multiverse across nine choices. Kirouane et al., vla-eval, IndustrialVLA-Bench, the SmolVLA ONNX study and VLAQuantBench are useful allies: independent 2026 evidence that the evaluation substrate changes results, which is the workshop's premise. Our single-step offline readout needs the standard caveat that offline action metrics do not predict closed-loop success (SIMPLER; REAL-I; SmolVLA ONNX). We should present the result as a property of the measurement, not as a deployment prediction. The reporting card can extend existing checklists (Kress-Gazit et al.; IndustrialVLA-Bench; vla-eval) with the items they lack: decoding rule, precision and kernel, token-to-bin map, prefix handling, action-axis sign and frame, mirror scoring, stimulus screening, the decisive statistic and its direction-awareness, and the wording set.

## (d) Gap evidence

Each statement lists the searches and sources behind it. "Found no paper" means none among the 1,081 distinct arXiv titles returned by the queries in (h) (titles screened, abstracts read where relevant), the full texts in (b)/(c), and the web searches listed.

1. **We found no paper that compares argmax and expected-value (or other) readouts of a VLA's action-token distribution, or varies bin width or the token-to-bin map, and reports the effect on a language-use verdict.** Queries: arXiv `decoding` (85 hits), `ovla_tokens` (7), `tokdist` (15), `ovla_lang` (53); web search "OpenVLA action token argmax versus expected value readout logits probe". Nearest: Grant et al. (2603.19233) note argmax fragility in an SAE-intervention context; MG-Select (arXiv 2510.05681) and INSIGHT (2510.01389) use token distributions for action selection or uncertainty, not as evaluation readouts; STAGE does not state its decoding rule.
2. **We found no paper that tests whether numerical precision or quantization changes a VLA language or grounding verdict.** Queries: `quant` (56), `quant_lang` (18). Precision studies report closed-loop success only: OpenVLA paper (4-bit ≈ bf16 on 8 Bridge tasks), VLAQuantBench (2609.25376), SmolVLA ONNX (2609.14146), bit-flip attacks (2608.15475).
3. **We found no paper that measures how instruction swaps change earlier-decoded action dimensions (the autoregressive prefix) in a token-based VLA.** Queries: `decoding`, `ovla_lang`, `interp` (276). Grant et al. mention error compounding across the 7-token sequence (SAE context only). Chen et al.'s "prefix" is the observation+instruction KV cache, not the action prefix.
4. **We found no VLA language evaluation that states, or varies, the axis and sign convention used to read a lateral action, or that treats image mirroring as a scoring choice.** Queries: `signconv` (4), `mirror` (8), `leftright` (9), `posbias` (6). Nearest: Act2Answer's left/right swap averaging and its effective-N caveat (2606.19297); vla-eval's action-mode and quaternion pitfalls (2603.13966); Hochedel & Lalonde (2606.30456); InstructMove/RoboIRGBench define the instruction frame, not the readout sign.
5. **We found no multiverse, specification-curve or many-analysts analysis of any robot-learning or VLA evaluation.** Queries: `multiverse_ro` (5 hits, none relevant: e.g. "PSG-Nav ... Multiverse Decision Making"), `multiverse_ml` (8), `mv2` (66), `evalsens` (10); web searches "multiverse analysis OR specification curve robot learning evaluation sensitivity" and "robot policy evaluation design choices change conclusions reproducibility audit". ML precedents exist (Bell et al. 2022; "One Model Many Scores", arXiv 2308.16681, fairness) but not in robotics. Robot-learning papers vary one choice at a time (Act2Answer App. A; STAGE threshold-free AUC; Lin et al. nine representations).
6. **We found no VLA language evaluation with identical twins placed on the same side of the gripper, and none that reads OpenVLA's actions on real robot frames with identical twins.** Queries: `identical` (17), `sameside` (1), `twins_vlm` (0), `refexp` (60), `ovla_bridge` (2), `fixedobs` (107). Nearest: LIBERO-CF real scene (three identical cups in a row, closed loop, π0.5); RefGuard (duplicates, closed loop, GR00T/π0.5 baselines); LangGap (same-category bowls, sim); STAGE (real Bridge frames only for Octo, no spatial swaps).
7. **We found no paper showing that a direction-blind contrast can score a backwards (anti-grounded) response as significant grounding in a VLA.** STAGE explicitly distinguishes direction-blind "action sensitivity" from direction-aware ActCheck and reports both, but does not show a verdict reversal; the Counterfactual Memory Audit (2609.27247) makes the analogous point for memory. Queries: `interp`, `fixedobs`, `evalsens`; full texts of STAGE and CMA abstract.
8. **We found no paper that reports the direction of a spatial word's effect flipping with instruction phrasing.** Wording sensitivity is widely documented (VLATest; LIBERO-Para; Q-DIG; Jeong et al.; VLA Grounder; Zhan et al.), always as success-rate changes, not as a sign change of a word effect. Queries: `vla_instr` (478 titles screened), `refexp`.

**Strongest defensible gap claim (for the paper):**
> To our knowledge, no prior evaluation of vision-language-action models varies the measurement choices that sit beneath a language-use verdict (which action slot is read and its sign, argmax versus expected-value readout and bin width, the token-to-bin map, numerical precision, the autoregressive prefix, the scoring of mirrored scenes, stimulus screening, the decisive statistic and the instruction wording) and reports which verdicts survive. Nor have we found a multiverse or specification-curve analysis of any robot-learning evaluation. Existing language evaluations fix one analysis path, check robustness to a single choice (a detection threshold, a layout swap, rendering conditions), or audit closed-loop success instruments and evaluation pipelines rather than readouts. We also know of no VLA language probe that uses identical objects on the same side of the gripper in real robot frames.

Hedge to keep: "to our knowledge, as of early October 2026", since 2026 output is very fast (e.g. four relevant papers appeared in the last week of September).

## (e) Threats: the closest works and exactly how we differ

1. **STAGE (Jin et al., arXiv 2609.13458; Findings of EMNLP 2026 per comment). Closest protocol.** Same design idea (fixed observation, swap only the instruction, read OpenVLA's single-step action), includes spatial-relation swaps, paraphrase-calibrated thresholds, a threshold-free AUC and a direction-aware metric (ActCheck), plus hidden-state probes and bootstrap CIs. *How we differ:* (i) STAGE's OpenVLA frames are LIBERO renders (its BridgeData V2 frames are used only with Octo and only for target/task changes); ours are real Bridge frames. (ii) STAGE uses distinct objects and anchor relations; we use identical twins, egocentric "left/right" and same-side controls that separate side-following from object-following. (iii) STAGE reads the decoded continuous action through one fixed pipeline (decoding rule unstated); we read the token distribution and vary readout, bin width, token map, precision and prefix. (iv) STAGE's conclusion is a model property ("semantic-action gap"); ours is a property of the measurement: which conclusions survive which choices. *Must concede:* STAGE already shows that direction-blind sensitivity and direction-aware consistency give different pictures (7.7% sensitivity vs "54.5% of native OpenVLA actions are target-aligned"), and it already checks threshold dependence.
2. **Kirouane et al. (arXiv 2609.07470). Closest thesis.** "The main challenge is measurement rather than translation": instruments that certify absent language following, null controls, seed replication, public retractions. *How we differ:* their instruments are closed-loop success benchmarks, a video-coherence metric, training loss and seeds, for Greek instructions on a Cosmos3 policy in simulation. Ours are inference-time readout and analysis choices for English spatial words on OpenVLA with real frames, organised as a multiverse with a pitfall/fork distinction. Cite them as converging evidence, not as prior art for our method.
3. **Act2Answer (Kachaev et al., arXiv 2606.19297). Closest on mirror scoring and evaluation-choice sensitivity.** Binary left/right choice, swap-averaged scoring, an explicit effective-N caveat for the two views, and an appendix testing whether "conclusions are sensitive to evaluation choices". *How we differ:* their ablations vary rendering, tile size, lighting, image resolution and prompt style one at a time and find conclusions unchanged; they do not vary readout, precision, prefix or statistic, they do not cross choices, and their task is knowledge (not spatial wording) in simulation.
4. **vla-eval (Choi et al., 2603.13966) and IndustrialVLA-Bench (Wang et al., 2609.25562). Closest on "pitfalls" and reporting.** Single undocumented parameters (state source, absolute vs delta actions, quaternion convention, centre crop) shift success by up to 55 points; evidence tiers and run-level seeds. *How we differ:* they audit closed-loop success pipelines across models; we audit a language verdict within one model. Their pitfalls are reproduction bugs; we separate pitfalls from legitimate forks and map the forks.
5. **LIBERO-CF (Fang et al., 2602.17659). Closest on twins.** Three identical cups with "middle/left/right" instructions on a real robot. *How we differ:* closed loop; three in a row (no same-side condition); a fine-tuned π0.5 with 20 demos for the in-domain target and one for each other target (training frequency confounded with the word); 10 trials per instruction; no action-level readout. LangGap (same-category LIBERO bowls), InstructMove (same-category distractor) and RefGuard (duplicate produce) are weaker versions of the same threat.
6. **Yang et al. (2609.22582). Closest in method spirit outside manipulation.** Edited real frames, open-loop readout of a VLA's planned action, pre-registered test of which verdicts transfer (left- vs right-hand drive). *How we differ:* driving, no language; the varied factor is the deployment domain, not the measurement pipeline.
7. **VLAQuantBench (2609.25376) and the SmolVLA ONNX study (2609.14146). Precision fork.** Both show precision and runtime choices change closed-loop success; the ONNX study also shows that a 16-token language width changes success. *How we differ:* neither looks at a language verdict; we show that 4-bit inference changes 58% of lateral argmax tokens but no headline conclusion, which places precision among the forks that matter little for this verdict.
